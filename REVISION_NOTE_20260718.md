# Revision note: evaluator-method reframing

This revision responds to a scientific-framing and reporting audit completed July 18, 2026.

## Main changes

- Reframed the paper around evaluator-dependent measurement rather than a model safety leaderboard.
- Replaced journal-specific front matter with a conventional structured abstract and IMRaD manuscript organization.
- Added the GPT-labelled rescored workbook to the frozen package.
- Demonstrated exact agreement between the GPT-labelled and Claude-labelled scoring-engine workbooks on all 15 score fields and all 1,200 rationales.
- Counted the scoring engine once to avoid pseudoreplication.
- Added evaluator-method prevalence, agreement, discordance, interaction, and duplicate-audit analyses.
- Added a dedicated figure and results-workbook sheets for the evaluator-method comparison.
- Expanded the clinical critique of the deterministic scoring engine and clarified that it detects lexical rule triggers rather than verified harm.
- Preserved the four-judge panel as the primary completed evaluation while labelling the cross-method comparison as post hoc and secondary.
- Added a leave-one-judge-out interaction analysis showing that the unsafe interaction was not detected after GPT-5.5 was excluded (7 remaining events; raw p=0.111), while it remained detected after exclusion of each other judge.

## Interpretation boundary

No evaluator stream is a physician reference standard. The study supports conclusions about measurement instability, evaluator threshold differences, and audience-associated patterns. It does not establish true clinical error prevalence or identify a clinically safest model.
